# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Shiplet serves teams who build with agents: engineers, designers, product
managers, and researchers who share agent-built work and the sessions behind it.
They use Claude Code, Codex, and other agents side by side. Reviewers work
directly on the artifact (build output, static exports, standalone files, and
public preview URLs) and read the agent session that produced it at the level
they were given. Teammates and their agents load that session and continue the
work, and CI systems collect the resulting tickets.

## Product Purpose

Shiplet makes what agents made, and how they made it, shareable. Artifacts get
a trusted review link with contextual feedback that keeps the revision it was
left on. Traces, the full agent sessions, are masked locally, private by
default, and shared per audience at Full, Conversation, or Summary level. Both
work across agents through an open trace format, MCP, and the CLI. Success means
a team never loses an agent session or its review context: anyone can publish
the work and the session, understand exactly who can see each, leave precise
feedback on a known version, and hand the work to the next person or agent,
without exposing credentials or mixing the artifact with the trusted host.

## Brand Personality

Quirky, practical, and crisp. Shiplet should feel like the front office of a
well-run working harbor: memorable and human, but as dependable and orderly as
infrastructure.

## Anti-references

- Generic blue SaaS dashboards and interchangeable admin chrome.
- Cinematic title screens, oversized decorative heroes, and spectacle that
  competes with the current task.
- Duplicate illustrative vessels in compact chrome; the Shiplet mark is the
  primary ship and should not compete with another featured boat.
- Nautical jargon in controls, cute metaphors that obscure meaning, decorative
  gradients, and visual clutter without product meaning.

## Design Principles

- Make the Shiplet mark the working vessel and primary maritime actor.
- Keep personality in surfaces, states, motion, and small details while labels
  remain plain and operational.
- Preserve one clear product task per viewport; compact chrome should support
  the work rather than become the work.
- Communicate trust through explicit boundaries, predictable controls, and
  visible system state.
- Earn realism through coherent working-harbor structure and believable motion,
  not ornamental spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast for text and controls, preserve visible keyboard focus,
use semantic native controls with associated labels, provide touch-sized mobile
targets, honor reduced-motion preferences, and never rely on color alone to
communicate state.
