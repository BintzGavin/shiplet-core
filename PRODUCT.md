# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Shiplet serves agent-first software teams reviewing build output, static exports,
standalone files, and public preview URLs. Human reviewers work directly on the
artifact while agents and CI systems collect the resulting tickets and continue
the implementation workflow.

## Product Purpose

Shiplet places artifacts inside a trusted review experience with explicit
identity, access policy, contextual feedback, and agent handoff. Success means a
team can publish a review, understand who can access it, leave precise feedback,
and move that feedback back into automated work without exposing credentials or
mixing the artifact with the trusted host.

## Brand Personality

Quirky, practical, and crisp. Shiplet should feel like the front office of a
well-run working harbor: memorable and human, but as dependable and orderly as
infrastructure.

## Anti-references

- Generic blue SaaS dashboards and interchangeable admin chrome.
- Cinematic title screens, oversized decorative heroes, and spectacle that
  competes with the current task.
- Duplicate illustrative vessels in compact chrome; the Shiplet mark is the
  primary ship and should not compete with another featured boat.
- Nautical jargon in controls, cute metaphors that obscure meaning, decorative
  gradients, and visual clutter without product meaning.

## Design Principles

- Make the Shiplet mark the working vessel and primary maritime actor.
- Keep personality in surfaces, states, motion, and small details while labels
  remain plain and operational.
- Preserve one clear product task per viewport; compact chrome should support
  the work rather than become the work.
- Communicate trust through explicit boundaries, predictable controls, and
  visible system state.
- Earn realism through coherent working-harbor structure and believable motion,
  not ornamental spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast for text and controls, preserve visible keyboard focus,
use semantic native controls with associated labels, provide touch-sized mobile
targets, honor reduced-motion preferences, and never rely on color alone to
communicate state.
